Research

A turbulent plume is not a smooth cloud: it meanders, breaks into filaments and reaches a sensor only in intermittent bursts. My research tackles the inverse problem of this stochastic transport: locating the source of a passive scalar from sparse, noisy and mostly empty observations. My PhD is built around four questions:

  • How can we infer the source of a passive scalar from binary detection events?
  • Which spatial and temporal correlations shape the statistics of plume detection?
  • How can cooperating agents use information theory to search efficiently?
  • Can gas sensing improve other localization modalities, such as radio frequency?

The application is finding victims in search and rescue missions with teams of drones. Supervised by Ahmed Boubrima and Hervé Rivano.
